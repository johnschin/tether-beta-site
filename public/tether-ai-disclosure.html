1. What Is Tether?

Tether is a single AI coaching tool deployed by employers to help their employees navigate organizational change. It is not a consumer app — it is a professional development tool accessed through your organization.

The platform is grounded in the ADKAR change management model (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement) and evidence-based psychological frameworks including Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), and motivational interviewing. These frameworks inform how Tether approaches coaching conversations about change uncertainty, adjustment, and adoption.

Tether was developed by Joree Rose, MA, LMFT, and Dr. John Schinnerer, PhD (UC Berkeley). Their professional training and clinical experience shaped the coaching frameworks, knowledge base, and safety protocols embedded in the platform. See Section 5 for important context about their roles.

2. You Are Interacting with Artificial Intelligence

Tether is powered by Claude, a large language model developed by Anthropic, Inc. This means:

  • You are talking to software, not a human being
  • No human at Tethered Consulting reads, reviews, or approves your individual responses in real time
  • The AI processes your message using: system instructions, the ADKAR-aligned coaching framework, a curated knowledge base drawn from change management research and applied psychology, and automated safety logic
  • The AI does not retain memory of previous sessions by default — each session starts fresh
  • Responses are generated probabilistically and may vary even for similar inputs

5. AI Limitations

Users should be aware of the following inherent limitations of AI coaching:

  • Hallucinations: AI can generate responses that are plausible-sounding but factually incorrect. Tether's outputs should not be relied upon as authoritative professional advice.
  • Incomplete context: The AI does not know your full situation — only what you share in the current session. It cannot access your employment history, medical records, or any other personal information.
  • Not personalized clinical advice: Responses are generated based on frameworks and patterns, not individual clinical assessment. Nothing Tether says constitutes medical, psychological, or legal advice.
  • Imperfect crisis detection: Safety protocols are automated and pattern-based. Tether may not recognize every crisis situation, particularly if distress is expressed indirectly or ambiguously. See Section 7.
  • No session continuity: By default, Tether does not carry memory from one session to the next. Each conversation begins without context from previous sessions.
  • Potential bias: Like all AI systems, Tether's responses may reflect limitations or biases present in training data. Use critical judgment.

6. Safety and Crisis Protocols

Tether employs an automated three-tier safety system designed to recognize when an employee's needs exceed what a coaching tool can provide and to redirect them to appropriate human support.

Tier 1 — Normal Coaching Range

The vast majority of interactions fall here. Stress about a reorg, frustration with a new manager, anxiety about job security, uncertainty during a system rollout — these are exactly what Tether is built for. The AI coaches normally: acknowledges, reframes, asks a focused question, suggests a next step.

Tier 2 — Soft Escalation

When an employee expresses hopelessness, feelings of being trapped, or prolonged despair that goes beyond situational work stress, Tether activates a soft escalation. The AI pauses coaching, acknowledges what was shared without judgment, states clearly that this goes beyond typical work stress, and refers the employee to their company's EAP as a confidential resource.

Tier 3 — Hard Escalation

When an employee mentions self-harm, suicidal thoughts, or intent to harm themselves or others, Tether stops coaching immediately. The AI expresses direct concern, provides the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, and instructs the employee to call 911 if in immediate danger. The coaching conversation does not continue.

Crisis and Emergency Resources

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: Call or text 988 (US, 24/7)

Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741

SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 (mental health and substance use, 24/7, free and confidential)

National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233

Emergency Services: Call 911

Do not rely on Tether to detect an emergency. If you or someone you know is in danger, call 911 immediately.

Safety protocol limitation: These protocols are automated and may not catch every at-risk situation. They are designed to help — they are not a substitute for human judgment, a licensed mental health professional, or emergency services.

Tether discloses at the start of each session, and on this page, that the user is interacting with an artificial intelligence system and not a human being. This disclosure is required by California AB 489 for automated decision systems and AI-powered services.

California SB 243 — Companion Chatbot Safety

California SB 243 (effective July 1, 2025) establishes safety requirements for AI systems that interact with users on personal topics. Tether complies through: (1) a three-tier safety protocol that redirects users to crisis resources when warranted; (2) automated logging of safety event activations (not conversation content); (3) annual anonymized reporting on safety event rates, beginning July 1, 2027; and (4) this AI disclosure page. Safety event logs are retained for a minimum of three years.
