For HR & L&D

You need to know how change is actually landing. Tether tells you.

Anonymized, aggregate intelligence on employee change readiness — stage by stage, team by team. Plus proactive coaching support that reaches every employee before they disengage.

Request a demo

Most organizations are flying blind through change.

Change programs generate a lot of activity — town halls, communications, training sessions. What they rarely generate is signal: honest, real-time data on how change is actually landing with individual employees.

By the time you have evidence that adoption is stalling — attrition upticks, engagement score drops, a manager flagging a team — the window to intervene has usually passed. Tether changes the data you have access to. Not by surveying employees about their experience (they've stopped responding to those). But by being the coaching tool employees actually use — and returning the aggregate patterns to you in real time.

What HR and L&D leaders see.

ADKAR stage distribution

Where your people are in the change journey.

At any moment, see where your employees are across the seven change stages — how many are still in Awareness, how many have reached Ability, where the largest concentrations of stuck employees are.

Emotional tone by team

Where anxiety is highest — and lowest.

Anonymized sentiment patterns across departments or business units. Not individual transcripts — aggregate signals that tell you where anxiety is highest and where it's lowest.

Engagement & usage patterns

Early signals before they become incidents.

Who is using Tether (at the cohort level), how often, and at what points in the change journey. Declining usage is an early signal worth paying attention to.

"Your employees won't tell you how they really feel about the change. But they will tell Tether."

What you see — and what you don't.

This distinction matters. Tether's value to HR depends entirely on employees trusting the tool enough to use it honestly. That trust requires a clear, unambiguous boundary: individual conversations are private. HR sees aggregate data only. Never individual transcripts, never session-level attribution, never a way to identify which employee said what.

This isn't just good ethics — it's good design. A coaching tool employees trust produces more honest signal than one they approach with suspicion. The anonymization isn't a limitation. It's what makes the data meaningful.
